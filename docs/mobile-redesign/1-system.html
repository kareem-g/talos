QAI Mobile · Redesign · Board 01

Console — the desktop’s dark palette, rebuilt for touch

QAI is a remote control for AI agents running on your desktop. The phone is not where you read a transcript; it is where you find out something needs you and act on it in seconds. So the design is built around one question — “does anything need me?” — and one verb: resolve it.

The colour is the desktop’s own: warm charcoal surfaces (canvas #131315, cards #26262B, plates #161618), parchment ink, and the desktop’s soft blue accent. What is new is the instrument geometry: rectilinear frames, hairline borders, mono readouts, a state rail and a fleet band. And no green, yellow or orange anywhere in the chrome — attention is carried by inverted paper ink and by contrast, not by hue.

3 · Geometry & signature rectilinear, hairline-framed, with a state rail and a fleet band

Rectilinear, hairline-framed

The old mobile language was full pills, soft fills and blurred chrome. The console is square-cornered and framed: 10px cards, 8px controls, 4px tags, 1px hairline borders, no blur, no glass. Elevation is a lighter surface, never a shadow — only sheets cast.

The signature: rail + band

Two structural devices encode state everywhere. A 3px rail on the left edge of every session card is the indicator light — paper ink when a human is required, accent blue when working, red when failed; a fleet band at the top of the Deck shows every session as a tick, blocked ones tallest and in paper ink. Both are live, both are tappable.

4 · Components one kit · one accent · states read as words

Buttons & chips

Affirmative actions are the accent blue, everywhere. Attention is not a colour: the blocked card gets a paper-ink rail, a lifted surface and the app’s only inverted white pill, while its resolving button stays the same accent primary as every other action. Filters are square chips; config reads as mono chips.

Status vocabulary

Colour is never the only channel — every state ships a word. Blocked states invert to paper ink and draw a larger dot; working is accent blue; failure is red; finished work goes grey. All eleven wire states, mapped to seven readable ones.

5 · Navigation architecture four destinations + one universal action

Structure

The desktop’s three-pane workspace becomes a tab root, a pushed session stack, and bottom-sheet panels. Nothing is a shrunken sidebar.
